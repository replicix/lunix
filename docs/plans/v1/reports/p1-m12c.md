**Every collector call runs under the coordinator mutex** on the calling thread (a call thread, the gate's
thread, the coordinator itself), through one helper (`with_collector`). It builds the view like the background
step (without cloning the retained commits), refuses `Busy` when the map is not at the tip, and publishes the
collector's status after the call. Every entry checks `closing` and the failed flag under that mutex.

**`run_pass(false)`** asks for a pass now (`request_pass`; `Unsupported` if the collector does not take
requests) and steps it to its end. Between two steps it releases the mutex and waits on a condvar: 5 ms after a
step that worked, 100 ms in the grace or after a compaction step that could do nothing (rate cap). So cuts, a
quiesce and the ladder get the mutex between steps, and no core spins.

Which pass answers:

- a pass already marking;
- for a pass past its mark (its view predates the call), the next one;
- an urgent pass that ends meanwhile (its mark began after the call).

A pass the collector drops is asked for again, four asks in all, then `Refused`. A map ahead of the tip is retried
for 50 steps, then `Busy`. **`run_pass(true)`** is one `Collector::dry_run`. **`run_urgent`** is one
`Collector::urgent`; its moves go to the next cut.

**Quiesce** sets `closing`, notifies the condvar, then takes the coordinator mutex for the final commit: no
collector call starts after it, and a waiting requested pass ends `Closed` at once. `closing` is one-way, also when
the final commit failed (documented on the field and on `Engine::quiesce`).

**Out of space.**

- Compaction re-hints are applied right after the destage succeeded (still before C5), so a cut whose destage
  fails leaves the map at the tip (review B-1).
- A commit that fails with `NoSpace` at any step (the destage, the retry of an unpersisted index run) runs the
  urgent pass on the coordinator, wanting the dirty bytes. It runs at least 2 s after the end of the last urgent
  pass of either kind; the pause doubles, up to 60 s, while those passes free nothing.
- A tick whose commit failed still steps the collector when the map is at the tip.

**The ladder.** `open_volume` registers `CompactStep { engine: Weak<Engine>, runs }`. It runs on the gate's thread
and calls `run_urgent(want)`; on a quiesced or dropped engine it frees 0. No deadlock: the coordinator never waits
for the gate (it only uses `reserve_headroom`, `release`, `transfer`), and a write parks in the gate before it
takes a `write_seq`, so a cut never waits for a parked write.

**Daemon.**

- `Gc` holds the volume mutex only to clone the engine. Not attached: `Invalid`. `Closed`: `Busy` ("detached or
  quiesced while the pass ran").
- `GcStatus` reads a `GcStatusHandle` (the engine's published status) kept per attached volume, set at attach and
  removed at detach. It holds no engine. Not attached: idle.
- Detach drops the device, then waits until no call holds the engine (`Gc`, the compact step; bounded at 30 s with
  a warning), then drops the stack. The engine's threads are stopped and `LOCK` is free when the detach answers.
- A non-dry `Gc` is exempt from `call_timeout` (it ends on detach or quiesce; the client's `--timeout` still
  applies).

## Deviations

- **`lunix-iface` changed** (two default methods on `Collector`): `GcControl::status` must not wait for the
  coordinator, and the engine names no implementation crate; a pass on request needs a way past the pass interval.
- **`Gc` does not hold the volume mutex for the pass** (the daemon's rule is one mutex per volume per call): a pass
  lasts at least the grace, and detach or `QuiesceAll` must not wait for it. Quiesce ends the call; detach waits for
  it to let go of the engine (one step at most).
- **The engine runs the urgent pass itself when a commit fails for space**, besides the gate's ladder, and applies
  re-hints after the destage: both are in the M11 coordinator (`coord.rs`).
- **`server.rs`**: a non-dry `Gc` waits without the `call_timeout`.
- **`tools/ci.sh`**: `stack_gc_ladder` and `control_gc` join the release stack tests.
