### What a crash is

`crash_with` drops the stack without a final commit, then:
1. **Journal past the flush point.** Every record (lane records and marks) above the flush point is kept, zeroed,
   torn from a 512-byte boundary, or has one byte flipped, at random and independently. The flush point is the larger
   of the driver's bound and the flushed watermark any surviving record or mark carries (ADR-4): nothing a completed
   group flush made durable is touched.
2. **Files never synced.** Every `*.tmp` and `*.open` below the volume directory (outside `journal/`, `cache/`) is
   deleted, truncated or kept at random.

The plan says "deletion of files not synced (the store's test hook)". The hook (`StoreOptions::fs`, an `FsOps` that
records renames and directory syncs) cannot be installed through `stack::OpenParams` (request R2). Every store object
becomes visible by rename plus directory `fsync` before anything names it, and the drop happens between calls, so the
unsynced set at a drop is the unsealed files above.

## Findings

### E1: `refuse` refuses damage that holds no missing number (lunix-engine, defect, failing test)

`lunix_engine::recover::refuse_worthy` refuses when any damaged range's `next_seq` is at or below the flushed
watermark. A damaged range can only hold the numbers between its lane's `prev_seq` and `next_seq`; when recovery
found every number above the floor (no gap), those numbers are either at or below the floor (they cost nothing,
on-disk-formats §5.3) or held by another lane's records. Such a range is harmless, yet it is refused. When a number
above the floor really is missing, recovery stops there (`gap_at`), and the gap rule (`gap_at <= flushed`) already
refuses; the range rule adds only false refusals.

How it happens with a crash alone: a commit covers journal records that no flush synced; a crash tears one of them;
recovery ignores it (below the floor) and the wipe zeroes only what follows the last record below the stop point, so
the torn record stays in the ring file before a later record of its lane; a later FLUSH raises the watermark past it;
the next recovery reports it with `next_seq <= flushed`, and `on_journal_damage = refuse` keeps the volume offline
although nothing is missing. The same holds for a range whose numbers sit in the other lane (seen at
`Encrypted 16 KiB seed 0x9f1c507187e22033 step 1472`: cut 915, flushed 917, a lane-0 range `909 .. 917` while lane 1
holds 916, no gap). The stack model test met this 4 821 times in 1.2 M ops (only in the `refuse` half); it tolerates exactly
this case (no gap at or below the watermark in the `wipe` recovery report), counts it and goes on with the `wipe` open.

Failing test: `cargo test -p lunixd --features testsupport --test stack_support -- --ignored`
(`refuse_ignores_torn_records_below_the_floor`). Proposed fix (lunix-engine, `recover.rs`): keep the duplicate and gap
rules and drop the range rule.

```diff
 fn refuse_worthy(rec: &lunix_iface::journal::Recovered) -> bool {
     let w = rec.flushed;
-    rec.duplicate.is_some()
-        || rec.gap_at.is_some_and(|g| g <= w)
-        || rec
-            .damaged
-            .iter()
-            .any(|d| d.next_seq.is_some_and(|n| n <= w))
+    // A damaged range holds only numbers between its lane's neighbours; a
+    // missing one above the floor stops recovery there (`gap_at`). Without
+    // such a gap the range held numbers at or below the floor or numbers
+    // another lane holds: it costs nothing (on-disk-formats §5.3).
+    rec.duplicate.is_some() || rec.gap_at.is_some_and(|g| g <= w)
 }
```
The engine's doc comment and its tests of the range rule (`journal_damage.rs`) change with it; the M12a
`wiring.rs::damage_followed_by_a_valid_record_is_refused` case has a gap (the damaged record's number is missing), so
it stays refused.

### F1: `lunix-fsck` does not know the mark ring (lunix-fsck, defect)

`lunix-fsck`'s journal scan (`journal.rs::parse_name`) accepts only `q<lane>-<ordinal>.j`. The mark ring
`m00-<ordinal>.j` (on-disk-formats §5, amendment 2026-11-14 (b)) gives `journal_file` ("not a ring file name") for
each of its 4 files on **every** volume, so a healthy volume never exits 0 (exit 1, warnings). Its marks are also left
out of the contiguity check, which can turn a mark's number into an apparent gap (`ignored_after_gap` warnings after a
kill, 75 to 84 per kill configuration). Every gate here therefore reads "clean" as **no finding of severity error** (exit < 2); the
warnings are listed. Proposed change: `parse_name` accepts `m00-<ordinal:08x>.j` as the mark ring
(`MARK_RING_LANE`), and the merge counts its marks for contiguity and the watermark as recovery does.

### F2: the index catch-up is not persisted by an idle close (observation)

After a kill between C6 (the commit is durable) and C8's index persist (`engine.c6.committed`, `engine.c7.*`,
`store.commit.after_rename`/`after_dirsync`, `store.state.*`, `store.checkpoint.*`, `index.before_manifest_swap`: 40
runs per configuration), recovery's index catch-up inserts the missing entries, but a clean close right after it commits nothing (`quiesce` is `Idle`), so the entries stay in the
memtable and `lunix-fsck` warns `index_missing` (its own text: "mount catches up"). Harmless (every mount catches up
again); `Engine::open` could persist a non-empty catch-up, or `quiesce` could cut once when the index is dirty.

### F3: no unsynced store file is ever present at a drop crash (observation)

`damage_unsynced` found no `*.tmp` or `*.open` file in 12 000 model crashes: at a drop between calls the store has no
unsealed object open (the destage is synchronous inside a commit). The unsynced-file half of the plan's crash only
matters inside a store operation, which is what the kill loops cover (`leftover` and `crash_leftover` findings after
the kill, all removed by recovery) and what R2's hook would make testable in process.

## Decisions taken (the lead confirms)

1. **One request at a time.** The driver submits sequentially so the oracle knows the `write_seq` order without
   engine hooks (the stack opens with `NoHooks`). Concurrency inside the engine is the M11 model test's; here the
   requests alternate between two queues, so lanes interleave.
2. **Commit coverage by cut.** A commit makes durable only the requests whose number is at or below its `cut_seq`
   (the driver's `issued()` bound per request); a grow's FULL commit covers everything before it.
3. **Recovered records are durable.** After a reopen the driver's bound starts at recovery's `next_seq - 1` (the first
   lane open syncs the kept records, on-disk-formats §5.2). Without this a second crash could damage records a
   previous recovery had made durable (the first stack model run failed on it).
4. **Refuse in half the sequences.** Crash damage lies above the watermark, so `refuse` must open; that checks the
   ADR-4 rule on the real journal (and found E1).
5. **Crash frequency.** About 1.5 % of the model steps stop the volume (1 % crash with damage, 0.2 % plain drop,
   0.3 % clean reopen; a 2 000-op sequence has about 30, plus the final crash and close), because every open scans every ring file whole (about
   0.4 s, see R3).
6. **Kill workload.** A prep child is killed in its first commit (`destage.after_segment_seal:1`: an unnamed sealed
   segment and a journal to replay), a `*.tmp` is planted, and the run child opens that (recovery with replay, sweep
   deletion and leftover removal), writes about 160 MiB over 12 commit rounds (ring files reclaimed and
   reinitialised), pauses after some flushes (journal marks), grows once, drops and reopens once, and closes.
   Every kill run starts from a fresh volume (copying a 256 MiB journal per run would cost more than the prep).
7. **"First, middle, last hit".** From a counting run of the same workload; a run that is not killed (timing-dependent
   counts: the journal marks) is retried at its own last hit, up to three times (once was not enough for
   `journal.mark.before_write` in one run: 40 hits counted, 39 then fewer).

## Spec gaps found

- on-disk-formats §5.3 says damage at or below the floor costs nothing, and the journal's wipe keeps such damage when
  a later record of its lane follows; neither the spec nor the `refuse` rule says what happens when the watermark
  later passes it (E1).
